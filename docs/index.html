The exact finite-population variance factor also controls a uniform quadratic MGF bound, and the optimal multiplier is determined by parity through a centered hypergeometric problem.

Theorem 1

The weighted finite-population bound

Let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_N)\in[a,b]^N\), let π be a uniform permutation, and let \(w\in\mathbb R^n\). For the centered statistic \(T_w=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i(X_{\pi(i)}-\bar X_N)\), define

\[ \log \mathbb E\,e^{tT_w} \le \frac{\kappa_N}{8}\,\rho_N(w)(b-a)^2t^2, \qquad \rho_N(w)=\frac{N\sum_i w_i^2-(\sum_i w_i)^2}{N(N-1)}. \]
Paper\(\rho_N(w)\)→rho N n w
Paper\(T_w\)→FinitePopulation.statistic X w σ
Lean certificate

Sharpness. The same development proves that κN is the smallest constant depending only on \(N\) that works uniformly over every bounded population, sample size, coefficient vector, and transform parameter.

The proof's four structural moves

A first reading path

The formal development follows the full chain from an arbitrary weighted population to the scalar hypergeometric problem, including the analytic work needed for the odd-population optimum.

  1. 1

    Reduce bounded populations to binary populations

    Convexity moves the permutation MGF from the interval cube to its endpoint vertices without restricting the signs of the weights.

  2. 2

    Reduce a centered slice maximizer to two levels

    Compactness supplies an extremizer; symmetric-polynomial identities and a three-coordinate argument show that one can be chosen with at most two coordinate values.

  3. 3

    Identify a centered hypergeometric MGF

    The two-level subset sum is exactly a scaled centered hypergeometric random variable, with the finite-population factor preserved in its variance.

  4. 4

    Run the sharp recursion and solve the parity split

    An exact derivative recursion lowers \((N,K,m)\) to \((N-2,K-1,m-1)\). Even \(N\) is fixed by local variance; odd \(N\) requires a sharp Bernoulli base and an integrated single-crossing argument.

The sharp coefficient

Why parity matters

\[ \kappa_N= \begin{cases} 1, & N \text{ even},\\[4pt] \displaystyle \frac{2}{N\log((N+1)/(N-1))}, & N \text{ odd}. \end{cases} \]

Even populations

An exactly balanced binary population forces the coefficient \(1\) through the second derivative at \(t=0\).

Odd populations

Exact balance is unavailable; the extremum is attained by the nearest-balanced Bernoulli slice at a nonzero tilt.

Consequences and trust

What is checked

Variance-scale Serfling correction

For equal weights, the MGF and one- and two-sided tail bounds use the exact sampling-variance factor \((N-n)/(N-1)\), multiplied by the sharp κN.

Weighted exchangeable transfer

The finite-population theorem transfers to bounded exchangeable vectors, including the smallest uniform constant and the zero-centered-weight degeneracy.

Kernel-checked scope

The source contains no sorry, admit, project-defined axiom, unsafe, implemented_by, or opaque declaration. The public audit reports only Lean and Mathlib's ordinary logical foundations.

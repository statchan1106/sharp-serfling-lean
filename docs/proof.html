The proof in mathematics and Lean

How the sharp bound is assembled

The proof has two levels of reduction and two levels of analysis: arbitrary weighted populations become two-level Hamming slices, those slices become centered hypergeometric MGFs, and a parity-sensitive induction determines the optimal constant.

1

Finite-population reduction

From arbitrary intervals to a Hamming slice

Zero-pad the coefficient vector to \(N\) coordinates and remove its constant component. This makes the statistic insensitive to the population mean and yields the exact identity

\[ \rho_N(w)=\frac{N}{N-1}\lVert w^\circ\rVert_2^2. \]

Affine normalization sends \(X\in[a,b]^N\) to the unit cube. Convexity of the permutation MGF then moves the maximum to a binary population. Conditioning on its number of ones turns the permutation average into a fixed-cardinality subset average.

Mathematics · exact chain

interval cube→ binary vertex→ K-subset→ centered slice MGF

The reduction preserves the centered-weight norm and introduces no sign restriction on \(w\).

2

Extremal geometry

Why a maximizer has only two coordinate values

Fix the centered Euclidean norm of the coefficient vector. Compactness gives a global maximizer of the slice MGF. The numerator is rewritten as an elementary symmetric polynomial, so any three coordinates can be isolated while the remaining coordinates enter through nonnegative coefficients.

On the circle cut out by fixed sum and fixed squared norm, first- and second-order conditions combine with a repeated-Rolle/Hermite sign argument. A strict maximum cannot have three distinct coordinates. Applying this to every triple forces a global maximizer with at most two levels.

Lee–Kim reduction cited in Section 3

\[ \max_{\substack{\mathbf 1^\top y=0\\ \lVert y\rVert_2^2=r^2}} \operatorname{MGF}_{N,K}(y) \quad\text{is attained by a vector with at most two values.} \]

3

Hypergeometric core

The recursion that retains the variance scale

A centered two-level slice is a scaled centered hypergeometric count \(H_{N,K,m}-Km/N\). Its MGF \(G_{N,K,m}\) obeys an exact derivative identity that removes one success, one sampled coordinate, and two population coordinates:

\[ G'_{N,K,m}(t) =2v_{N,K,m}e^{r_{N,K,m}t}\sinh(t/2) G_{N-2,K-1,m-1}(t), \] \[ v_{N,K,m}=\frac{K(N-K)m(N-m)}{N^2(N-1)}. \]

The prefactor is exactly the hypergeometric variance. That identity is the reason the sample-size term \(m(N-m)/(N-1)\) survives the induction instead of being replaced by a looser finite-population correction.

Paper · Theorem 2

\[ \log G_{N,K,m}(t) \le \kappa_N\frac{m(N-m)}{8(N-1)}t^2. \]

5

Sharpness and assembly

Return from the scalar problem to the main theorem

The variational constant is defined as the maximum over sample size and success count, followed by the supremum over nonzero tilt. The upper bound comes from sharp_mgf; the matching lower bound is local for even \(N\) and attained at nonzero tilt for odd \(N\).

\[ \kappa_N^\star = \max_{1\le m\le N-1}\max_{0\le K\le N} \sup_{t\ne0} \frac{8(N-1)}{m(N-m)t^2}\log G_{N,K,m}(t) =\kappa_N. \]
